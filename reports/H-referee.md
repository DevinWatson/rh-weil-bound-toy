## 4. The four specific questions (task 4)

(a) Even k: can a class in an even-shifted degree with eigenvalue q^{k/2 - 1} cancel the balanced products, and does the argument need them at all?

- Balanced products. For even k, A contains products of k eigenvalues that pair up as alpha * (q/alpha) and equal q^{k/2}; this uses only the functional equation alpha <-> q/alpha, not RH. In M^k they appear with the sign (-1)^k = +. In q N a class of H^i_c(Y,K) with eigenvalue q^{k/2 - 1} enters as q * q^{k/2 - 1} = q^{k/2} with sign (-1)^i. Hence in R = q N - M^k the coefficient of q^{k/2} is (number of even-i classes) - (number of odd-i classes) - (number of balanced products). A class in even degree i (necessarily i >= k, since i >= k-1 by Artin vanishing under non-characteristic restriction and k - 1 is odd; weight k - 2 <= i is allowed) can cancel: yes, in principle. t = 1 is the explicit example of this phenomenon one degree up (top class, eigenvalue q^{k-1}, cancels q^k of M^k).
- Does the argument need balanced products? No. Only the pure power gamma* = alpha_*^k of the largest-modulus alpha_* enters Lemma L, and in the hypothetical world where the bound fails (|alpha_*| > sqrt q) gamma* is not balanced. What the argument needs is that no even-degree class has eigenvalue alpha_*^k / q (or more precisely that the signed multiplicity does not cancel). In the real world, weights forbid this for modulus reasons, but the argument must not use weights, so it has to forbid it by a vanishing theorem, which is (S_k) again. So the balanced products are a red herring for the logic but they are a good test of (S_k) in the real world: if even-degree classes of eigenvalue q^{k/2-1} exist, (S_k) fails. For k = 4, N_4's exact expansion has the constant exponential t0^4 = +1 with the wrong sign (3.4), which (S_4) needs to cancel against a -1 in E; I could not test that.

(b) Does any step use the Weil bound, the Weil conjectures or weights?

- Steps 1.1 to 1.4 (identity, Parseval, Lemma L, exponent): no. Elementary, as I rederived them. The trace formula, Kunneth, Euler-Poincare: no weights (CITED). So the skeleton of the argument is not circular.

The only known ways to obtain the second half of (S_k) (concentration in the middle degree, equivalently no geometric coinvariants and no sections at h = 0 in the decomposition of the sheaf of S(h) over the h-line, which I derived: S_k is equivalent to fibrewise concentration plus H^0_c(A^1_h, F) = H^2_c(A^1_h, F) = 0) use irreducibility or big monodromy (Katz, Deligne's equidistribution), proved via the second moment and Weil II purity, which is the weight statement. A weight-based proof of (S_k) for the pair (Y, K) would contain the toy theorem for the restriction of K to a coordinate line, i.e. be circular (A makes the same point). I found no weight-free proof for k >= 4, and the footnote gives none. HINT (literature recollection, not checked against the sources).

(c) Is Artin vanishing applied to a sheaf for which it holds?

- Artin vanishing (SGA 4 XIV 3.1; BBD 4.1.1): for an affine variety Y and perverse P, H^i(Y,P) = 0 for i > 0 and H^i_c(Y,P) = 0 for i < 0 (the second by duality). It applies to P = K|_Y[k-1] only if that is perverse, which holds if Y is non-characteristic for K (and Y is lci, so a lisse rank-one sheaf is fine at the vertex of the cone when f(0) != 0). Then the lower half of (S_k) follows: H^i_c(Y,K) = 0 for i < k-1. That application is legitimate (for the sheaf K = (j_! L)^{boxtimes k}, j_! = j_* because L has monodromy -1 at each root). It gives nothing for i > k-1; the upper half is not an Artin statement.
- So "definite sign from middle cohomology" does not follow from Artin vanishing. In the Lefschetz picture (the footnote's "one off the one on M^k": total degree k + 1 for the middle class of the (x,h)-space against degree k for the h = 0 slice), the missing half is a generic-vanishing statement for rank-one tame sheaves on the complement of the quadric (or on the fibres Q = s). The torsion character chi is the standard exceptional case of such theorems, and for even k the sheaf K is radially unramified at infinity on the generic point of the boundary quadric (monodromy chi^{kd} = 1 when k is even and d is odd), which is the feature that makes classes survive for t = 1 (HINT, my reading). For odd k the radial monodromy is chi (nontrivial), the opposite situation, but odd k fails non-characteristic restriction (1.9).
- Non-characteristic restriction does hold at finite strata for generic roots and generic c, for even k <= 6, d = 3 (CHECKED, exact, over Q), but it fails in the following cases, which are exactly the cases of the tests run so far.

- Poonen's Bertini (CITED, Ann. of Math. 160, 2004) is about hypersurfaces of large degree and does not apply to a fixed quadric shape; the Goresky-MacPherson / Kiehl-Weissauer perverse Lefschetz theorems apply over an algebraically closed field, so over F_q one only gets existence of good c over Fbar_q, and the extension of F_q above is how to turn it into F_{q^m}-points.
