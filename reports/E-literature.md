## 1. Is footnote 3 known?
I found NO source containing the footnote 3 argument (sum over h first, path quadric x1x2+...+x_{k-1}x_k, sign of middle cohomology against M^k, iterated Parseval). A handful of searches finding nothing is not evidence of novelty. OPEN.

Related known routes, and how each differs (all attributions HINT unless said):

(a) Positivity / Rankin trick. Deligne, Weil I (Publ. IHES 43, 1974) uses Rankin's squaring method: non-negativity of a Hermitian square (a point count of a product with its conjugate) in high tensor powers bounds the weights of poles. Katz, "L-functions and monodromy: four lectures on Weil II", Adv. Math. 160 (2001) 81-132, applies Rankin squaring to a family with big monodromy (summary of the notes at https://www.math.arizona.edu/~swc/notes/files/00KatzN.pdf; bibliography https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/). Katz also gives an elementary proof of RH for smooth hypersurfaces building on Deligne's reading of Rankin (https://arxiv.org/pdf/math.NT/0504316, snippet). Difference: Rankin uses a positive-definite quantity (|.|^2) and tensor-power growth; footnote 3 claims opposite cohomological signs of two eigenvalue families, a statement about signs of multiplicities. Katz's "an inch" remark: not located. OPEN.

(b) Auxiliary quadrics / Gabber. No source found for a Gabber-hypersurface argument of the kind footnote 3 invokes. The only Gabber hit was Katz using Gabber's classification of subgroups of SO(7), unrelated. OPEN.

(c) Sums of products of trace functions. Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel, "A study in sums of products", https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.2293 (uses Goursat-Kolchin-Ribet criterion and Deligne's RH as input); Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel-Sawin, "Bilinear forms with trace functions", https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459. Difference: they derive cancellation FROM Deligne's theorem; footnote 3 claims to derive Weil/Deligne. A question for the mathematics reports, not settled here: bounding R by iterated Parseval needs square-root bounds on Fourier transforms of t(x)=chi(f(x)), which is itself Weil-strength input (possible circularity). OPEN. Hooley and Bombieri on this theme: nothing retrieved. OPEN. Katz, "Sommes exponentielles": not retrieved. OPEN.

(d) Stepanov. Stepanov 1971 for Kloosterman sums (https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/IM2_1971_5_2_a3), generalised by Bombieri to arbitrary curves; an elementary auxiliary-polynomial argument that uses vanishing to high multiplicity (summary: https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-03650310v1/file/Chicharro.pdf). Difference: no cohomology, multiplicity is the engine. Footnote 3 is cohomological and sign-based, so I see no technical descent from Stepanov; "descendant" in the note is a narrative claim. Kopparty's course notes (hit in search, https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sk1233/courses/finitefields-F13/weil.pdf) also treat the Stepanov case.

## 2. Standard theorems the argument would need
- Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula and rationality: Grothendieck, SGA 5 (1965-66); Deligne, SGA 4 1/2 "Rapport" and "Fonctions L modulo l^n et modulo p". Cited by name from memory; not opened.
- Artin vanishing: SGA 4 Exp. XIV 3.1; Beilinson-Bernstein-Deligne, "Faisceaux pervers", Asterisque 100 (1982), Theoreme 4.1.1. A 2026 paper (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20187) cites both for: affine pi, R pi_! F perverse coconnective for F perverse coconnective. Another snippet (BBDG 4.1.1) states Rf_* right t-exact and f_! left t-exact for f affine. The two wordings differ, so check the convention in BBD. On an affine variety Y and perverse Q: H^r(Y,Q)=0 for r>0 and H^r_c(Y,Q)=0 for r<0 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0660). HINT.
- Weak Lefschetz for perverse sheaves, generic hyperplane section: Deligne's perverse weak Lefschetz, cited in the 2601.20187 snippet; the same paper and https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0660 stress the embedding must be affine (punctured plane counterexample). HINT.
- Euler-Poincare formula (Grothendieck-Ogg-Shafarevich for curves; higher-dimensional forms by Deligne, Laumon, Saito): names only, exact form not retrieved. OPEN.
- Non-characteristic restriction (Deligne-Laumon, SGA 7 XIII; Saito's singular support theory): names only. OPEN.
- Not opened: Weil I, Weil II (Publ. IHES 52, 1980), Weil 1948.
