## Where things stand

Setting: q odd, f squarefree of odd degree d >= 3 over F_q, f(0) != 0, chi the quadratic character, t = chi(f),
L the rank-one sheaf of chi(f) on the affine line (extended by zero at the roots Z of f), M = sum of t.
Q_c(x) = c_1 x_1 x_2 + ... + c_{k-1} x_{k-1} x_k, Y = {Q_c = 0} in affine k-space, K = restriction to Y of the
external product of k copies of L, N = sum over Y(F_q) of t(x_1)...t(x_k), and q N = M^k + R.

Established in round one (see TOY_CASE.md for labels and files):
- q N = M^k + R, |R| < q^((k+3)/2), and "if alpha^k is not cancelled in R then |alpha| <= q^(1/2 + 3/(2k))".
  The Parseval part and the no-cancellation endgame are in Lean.
- Lower half: H^j_c(Y, K) = 0 for j < k-1, for every k, f, c. (K[k-1] is perverse on the affine hypersurface Y.)
- k = 2: concentration in degree 1 for every c. k = 3: concentration in degree 2 unless f(x) f(-(c_1/c_2) x) is a
  square; it fails for c_1 = -c_2.
- k = 4 (report I): Y is the cone of 2x2 matrices of rank <= 1, a family of planes over the projective line with
  parameter r. With C(r) = sum over x of t(x) t(r x) and tau(r) = -(c_3/c_1)(r + e), e = c_2/c_3:
      N_4 = -q + t_0^2 M^2 + sum over r of C(r) C(tau(r)).
  H^5_c = H^6_c = 0 for every c. If c_1 + c_2 + c_3 = 0 the plane {x_1 = x_3, x_2 = x_4} lies in Y and
  H^4_c != 0 with eigenvalue q^2: the sign statement FAILS there. For c_1 + c_2 + c_3 != 0, H^4_c is the
  coinvariants of V(r) tensor V(tau r) on the r-line (modulo a differential), V(r) = H^1_c of the line with
  coefficients in the sheaf of chi(f(x) f(r x)). OPEN.
- Data: for generic f and c the sums N_k have size about p^((k-1)/2) for k = 4, 5, 6; with c_1+c_2+c_3 = 0 at
  k = 4, N_4 is about +p^2.

## The open statement

(S_k) H^j_c(Y, K) = 0 for j > k-1, for c outside an explicit bad set. The argument needs less: (W*) the coefficient
of alpha^k (alpha an eigenvalue of largest modulus) in q N_n - M_n^k is nonzero, for infinitely many k.

No proof may use weights, purity, the Weil bound or the Weil conjectures: the target is the Weil bound, so that
would be circular. Weights may be used to interpret data, and you must say when you do.

## Suggestions from the main session (NOT checked: verify or refute each before using it)

S-a. Forget supports. For a perverse sheaf P on an affine variety, Artin vanishing gives H^j_c = 0 for j < 0 and
     H^j = 0 for j > 0. So if the natural map from compactly supported to ordinary cohomology is an isomorphism for
     (Y, K[k-1]), then (S_k) holds. No duality inside Y is needed, so singular points of Y do not matter; only the
     behaviour of K at infinity does.
S-b. Where it is not clean at infinity. In the closure of Y in the product of k projective lines, the locus
     {x_a = x_b = infinity} for non-adjacent a, b lies in the closure and is a divisor of it, and the monodromy of
     K around that divisor is (-1)(-1) = +1. So K extends across it and forget-supports fails there. The correction
     is the cohomology of that divisor with an explicit rank-one sheaf. Any correct theory must reproduce the
     failure at k = 4, c_1 + c_2 + c_3 = 0, and no failure for generic c.
S-c. A lemma for k = 4. Let A and B be lisse sheaves on open subsets of the affine line, tame everywhere
     (including infinity), whose finite singular sets are disjoint. Then every map A -> B on the common open set
     factors through a geometrically constant quotient of A: it kills (g - 1)A for every g in an inertia group at a
     singular point of A, because B is unramified there, and the quotient of the tame fundamental group by the
     normal subgroup generated by the inertia groups at all finite points is the tame fundamental group of the
     affine line, which is trivial. So Hom(V, tau^* V-dual) is controlled by the constant quotients of V alone
     whenever the singular sets of V and tau^* V are disjoint, which holds for all but finitely many tau.
S-d. Constant quotients of V. The total space {(x, r)} with the sheaf of chi(f(x) f(r x)) becomes a product
     after the substitution y = r x on x != 0, plus the line x = 0. That bounds H^3_c of the total space, hence the
     coinvariants of V on the r-line, with a possible contribution at r = 1 where f(x) f(r x) is a square.
S-e. Fibration over h (TOY_CASE.md section 9): only the degrees k-1, k, k+1 can occur, the k-fold products of
     eigenvalues inject into the middle cohomology of R, and the wrong-parity group is the coinvariants in the h
     direction of one local system.
