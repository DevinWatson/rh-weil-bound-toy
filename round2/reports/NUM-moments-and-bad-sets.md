### 2.1 The linear conditions (PROVED, elementary)
Take a perfect matching sigma of {1..6} and the subspace x_i = x_sigma(i), dimension 3, coordinates y_1, y_2, y_3 (one per pair). The term c_i x_i x_{i+1} becomes c_i y_a y_b, where a and b are the pairs of i and i+1 (a monomial y_a^2 if i and i+1 are matched to each other). Q_c vanishes identically on the subspace iff, for each pair of pairs {a,b}, the sum of the c_i over the edges (i,i+1) joining a and b is 0, and, for an edge inside a pair, its c_i is 0. On the subspace the weight is the product of t(y_a)^2, so the sum over the subspace is (p-r0)^3. All 15 matchings with their conditions are in `m2_matchings.txt`. The five matchings with no adjacent pair (the brief's set) and their conditions:
- 1-3, 2-5, 4-6: c1+c2 = 0, c3 = 0, c4+c5 = 0
- 1-4, 2-5, 3-6: c1+c4 = 0, c3 = 0, c2+c5 = 0
- 1-4, 2-6, 3-5: c1 = 0, c3+c4 = 0, c2+c5 = 0
- 1-5, 2-4, 3-6: c1+c4 = 0, c5 = 0, c2+c3 = 0
- 1-6, 2-4, 3-5: c1 = 0, c5 = 0, c2+c3+c4 = 0
Two of the five force c1 = 0. Under the normalisation c1 = 1 they are empty (for c1 = 0, x1 does not occur in Q and N = M N_5). The other three are the one-parameter families A: c = (1,-1,0,u,-u); B: c = (1,u,0,-1,-u); D: c = (1,u,-u,-1,0). Their union has 3p-2 vectors (CHECKED: 31, 37, 49 vectors for p = 11, 13, 17 by enumeration over all p^4 vectors, `m2_predicted.tsv`, and the families generated from these formulas lie in the enumerated set). ## 3. M3: first and second moments over h, k = 2..8

Definitions (as in the task). A_1(k,c,p) = (p N_k(0) - M^k) / ((p-1) p^(k/2)) and A_2(k,c,p) = (p sum_s N_k(s)^2 - M^(2k)) / ((p-1) p^k). Algebra (PROVED, Plancherel and orthogonality): sum_s N_k(s) = M^k, N_k(0) = (1/p) sum_h S(h), sum_s N_k(s)^2 = (1/p) sum_h |S(h)|^2, so (p-1)p^(k/2) A_1 = sum_{h != 0} S(h) and A_2 = (1/(p-1)) sum_{h != 0} |S(h)|^2 / p^k: A_1 is the mean of S(h)/p^(k/2) over h != 0 and A_2 the mean of |S(h)|^2/p^k. Here S(h) = sum over x of prod t(x_i) psi(h Q_c(x)).

Scripts and files: `m3_moments.py POLY K PMAX BUDGET` writes `m3_<poly>_k<K>.tsv` with, for every good prime and each of the three c types, exact M, N_k(0), sum_s N_k(s)^2, the exact mean of S(h) (a Fraction R/(p-1)), exact A_1 (a Fraction for even k; for odd k only R/(p-1) is rational and A_1 is given as a float hint), exact A_2 (a Fraction) and floats. Each row passed the exact check sum_s N_k(s) = M^k (asserted in the script). c types: `ones` (c = (1,...,1)); `rand1`, `rand2` (entries uniform in F_p^*, `random.Random("<poly>-<k>-<p>-rand1")`, `...rand2`). Analysis: `m3_analyze.py` -> `m3_summary.txt`, `m3c_fit.py` -> `m3c_fit.txt`, `m3d_a2_decay.py` -> `m3d_a2_decay.txt`, `m3b_pal.py` -> `m3b_pal.tsv`, `m3b_pal_summary.txt` (palindromic random c), `m3e_plane_A1.py` -> `m3e_plane_A1.txt`. ### 3.1 A_1 (CHECKED trend)
- A_1 is small and of both signs for every k and every c type: for p in [301, 700] f1 k = 4: mean A_1 = -0.001 (ones), +0.003, +0.001; mean |A_1| 0.082, 0.068, 0.061, max |A_1| 0.299 (n = 61 primes per c type). k = 6, p in [301,700]: mean |A_1| 0.055, 0.037, 0.034 (`m3_summary.txt`). Over the whole table, the windows of p show mean |A_1| falling from about 0.3 to 0.55 (p <= 50) to about 0.03 to 0.17 (p > 300).
- Rate: the least-squares log-log slope of binned mean |A_1| against p is between -0.30 and -0.69 for all (poly, k) (f1: -0.48 k=4, -0.46 k=5, -0.60 k=6, -0.63 k=7, -0.57 k=8; `m3c_fit.txt`), i.e. consistent with |A_1| of order p^(-1/2) (HINT; bins of 8 primes, 7 to 14 bins; not a proof and not a fit to a model). At p = 677 and k = 4 the last-bin mean |A_1| is 0.068.
- So the data say: no constant quotient of top size in the h direction for generic c, at least up to p = 677 (k = 4), 601 (k = 6), 461 (k = 7, 8). - Parity: odd k behaves like even k (A_1 floats for k = 5, 7 are of the same size and decay as for k = 4, 6, 8). For odd k, p^(k/2) is irrational; the exact rational statement is for the mean of S(h), R/(p-1), in the TSV.

### 3.2 A_2 (CHECKED trend; reading by weights stated where used)
- Exact values are Fractions in the TSVs. Integrality: p-1 divides p sum N^2 - M^(2k) in only 136 of 2985 rows, so A_2 is not an integer at finite p (as expected: it is a ratio with denominator (p-1) p^k).
- c random: A_2 hovers around 1. For f1, p >= 200: mean A_2 = 0.998 (k=4, n=77 primes, rand1), 0.988 (rand2); k=6: 0.972, 0.979 (n=64); k=5, 7, 8 between 0.95 and 1.00 (`m3d_a2_decay.txt`, `m3_summary.txt`). The mean |A_2 - 1| falls from 0.27 to 0.52 in the first bin of 8 primes (p from 11 to about 40) to 0.03 to 0.09 in the last bin, with log-log slopes -0.44 to -0.79 (HINT; `m3d_a2_decay.txt`; at k = 2 the random-c rows equal the `ones` rows because there is one coefficient, so ignore the k = 2 'target 1' lines).
- c = (1,...,1): A_2 hovers around 2, not 1: f1 p >= 200: mean 1.994 (k=4), 2.014 (k=5), 1.973 (k=6), 2.003 (k=7), 1.942 (k=8). The reason is a symmetry: the all-ones path form is invariant under reversal x_i <-> x_(k+1-i). I tested this: random PALINDROMIC c (c_i = c_(k-i), entries uniform) give A_2 about 1.9 for p in [151, 311] at all k = 4..8 (`m3b_pal_summary.txt`: means 1.905, 1.902, 1.893, 1.969, 1.957), and random c that happen to be palindromic by chance in the random sample show it too: k = 4, c = (162, 26, 162) at p = 179, A_2 = 1.854; c = (77, 19, 77) at p = 79, A_2 = 1.583; c = (78, 1, 78) at p = 331, A_2 = 1.780. The k = 2 and k = 3 (c1 = c2) cases give the same 2.
- Question: does A_2 tend to an integer? The data are consistent with A_2 -> 1 for c without reversal symmetry and A_2 -> 2 for reversal-symmetric c, for k = 2..8, with deviation decaying roughly like p^(-1/2) to p^(-3/4). That is a statement about averages over p-windows and about p up to 400 to 700, and it is a trend, not a limit. INTERPRETATION (uses weights): A_2 is a mean square of traces of Frobenius, normalised by p^k; for a pure sheaf of weight k that is geometrically irreducible the mean square tends to 1, and for a sheaf that is a direct sum of two irreducible pieces to 2. I cannot read the integer from the data without that interpretation, and I did not prove anything about it.
- Link with M2 (PROVED in section 2.2, item 4): sum_s N_k(c; s)^2 = N_{2k}(c, 0, -c). For random c at k = 3, A_2 = 1 predicts N_6/p^3 about 1 on family B, which is what M2 observed (0.9 to 1.06).
- Heavy tails remain at small p: for random c at p <= 100 values up to 1.5 to 1.9 occur (e.g. f1 k=4 rand2 p = 79: 1.583, which is a palindromic c, see above; f2 k=4 p=67: 1.358; f4 k=7 p=53: 1.468) and are consistent with the O(p^(-1/2)) fluctuation.

## 4. M4: odd k (k = 5, 7) versus even k

Scripts: `m4_odd.py` (writes `m4_odd_summary.txt`, `m4a_random.tsv`, `m4b_k5_exhaustive.tsv`; this run took about 10 minutes, at the cap), `m4c_robust.py` -> `m4c_robust.txt`, `m4d_outliers.py` -> `m4d_outliers.txt`.

1. The degeneracy (PROVED over Q by sympy, `m4_odd_summary.txt`, and elementary): the Gram matrix of Q_c has determinant c1^2 c3^2 (k=4), -c1^2 c3^2 c5^2 (k=6) for even k, and 0 for odd k (k = 3, 5, 7, 9 checked symbolically; the matrix is tridiagonal with zero diagonal, so odd size gives determinant 0). For odd k the radical is one-dimensional, with r_even = 0, and (sympy, normalised r_k = 1) is: k=3: (-c2/c1, 0, 1); k=5: (c2 c4/(c1 c3), 0, -c4/c3, 0, 1); k=7: (-c2 c4 c6/(c1 c3 c5), 0, c4 c6/(c3 c5), 0, -c6/c5, 0, 1). So Q_c(x + lambda r) = Q_c(x): Y is a cylinder in the direction r, and the sum over the lines x + lambda r of the weights prod t(x_i + lambda r_i) (for odd i) times the fixed weights of the even coordinates is a character sum in lambda of a polynomial of degree (k+1)/2 times f. This is the structural difference from even k; its size for generic c is what the next items measure.
2. Size of N_k(0) for generic c (CHECKED, `m4a_random.tsv`): for f1, f2, f4, primes 61..311, 10 random c per (poly, prime) (entries uniform in F_p^*, seeds `m4a-<poly>-<k>-<p>-<j>`), k = 3..8, 1400 samples each. With E = N_k(0) - M^k/p (exact) and Z = E / p^((k-1)/2):
   - median |Z|: 0.478 (k=3), 1.172 (4), 0.905 (5), 0.689 (6), 0.661 (7), 0.658 (8);
   - RMS Z for p >= 150: 1.73, 1.93, 1.45, 1.03, 0.99, 0.99; RMS Z excluding the top 1% of |Z|: 1.45, 1.62, 1.37, 1.00, 0.96, 0.94 (`m4c_robust.txt`);
   - fraction of Z > 0: 0.543, 0.551, 0.514, 0.483, 0.502, 0.498.
   So E has size p^((k-1)/2) for generic c at every k from 3 to 8, with no alternation between odd and even k: k = 5 and k = 7 sit on the smooth curve through k = 4, 6, 8 (the spread decreases with k: 1.9 at k = 4, 1.45 at k = 5, 1.03 at k = 6, then 0.99 at k = 7 and 8). The k = 6, 7, 8 values (median about 0.66, RMS about 1.0) are what a standard Gaussian would give (median 0.674); that is a descriptive remark (HINT), not a claim about the distribution.
   - Mean Z is within 0.04 of 0 at k = 5..8; the k = 3, 4 means (+0.14, +0.28) are driven by the rare exceptional c (next item).
3. Exceptional c at small k (CHECKED against the regenerated c, `m4d_outliers.txt`): in the random samples, the vanishing blocks c1+c2 = 0 (k=3) and c1+c2+c3 = 0 (k=4) appear in 6 of the 53 outliers |Z| > 4 at k = 3 and in 8 of the 29 at k = 4, against baseline frequencies 0.004 and 0.021 over all samples. The other outliers at k = 3, 4 are the heavy tail of the closed forms (k = 3: N_3 = t0 M^2 + (M - t0) C(r), product of two O(sqrt p) quantities divided by p). For k = 5..8 the outliers (|Z| > 3 at k = 5, > 2.5 at k = 6, 7, 8) are not explained by any vanishing block sum of length >= 2: 4 of 44 at k = 5 (baseline 0.034), 5 of 20 at k = 6 (0.066), 3 of 14 at k = 7 (0.090), 2 of 14 at k = 8 (0.136). No condition found. The family-B vectors of M2 (c3 = 0) cannot appear in these samples because entries were drawn nonzero.
4. What exceptional means for odd k (CHECKED + reasoning): for even k the matching subspaces have dimension k/2 and contribute (p-r0)^(k/2), larger than the generic size p^((k-1)/2) by a factor p^(1/2), so a threshold N/p^(k/2) >= 1/2 isolates special c (section 1 and 2). For odd k the analogous subspaces have dimension (k-1)/2 (one coordinate forced to 0 and a matching of the others) and contribute of order p^((k-1)/2), the same order as generic N_k; there is no gap, and the corresponding threshold N/p^((k-1)/2) >= 1/2 is met by 16% to 34% of all c (more where |M| is near 2 sqrt p): k = 5, c = (1,c2,c3,c4) exhaustive at p = 11, 13, 17 (`m4b_k5_exhaustive.tsv`): fraction with 2N >= p^2 is 403/1331 and 552/2197 and 1443/4913 for f1 at p = 11, 13, 17; 447/1331, 539/2197 for f2; 356/2197 for f3 at 13; 488/2197 for f4 at 13 (with larger fractions where |M| is near 2 sqrt p, e.g. f3 p = 17: 4905 of 4913). My attempt to explain these by subspaces of the form (coordinate = 0) + (matching) was not informative: the union of the 15 subspaces contains 38%, 33% and 26% of all c (503 of 1331 at p=11, 723 of 2197 at 13, 1283 of 4913 at 17), so I make no claim from that test. Conclusion: at odd k the route has no clean separation of a 'bad set' from the bulk at the scale of the main term, so (S_k)-type statements cannot be tested by counting bad c as at even k; one has to look at E = N - M^k/p instead (item 2), and for odd k the correct reference size is p^((k-1)/2), not p^(k/2).
5. Caveat on item 2 (CHECKED): the raw N_k(0), unlike E, is dominated by the main term M^k/p at k = 7, 8 for these p: RMS of M^k/p^((k+1)/2) over the same (poly,p) pairs is 0.16, 0.25, 0.42, 0.72, 1.26, 2.2 for k = 3..8 (`m4c_robust.txt`), so the RMS of N/p^((k-1)/2) is 1.62 (k=7) and 2.43 (k=8) while that of E is 0.99. 'The sums N_k have size about p^((k-1)/2)' is true for the excess E, and for N itself only while M^k/p is small.
6. Not done: no odd-k analogue of the closed form of report I (N_5, N_7 as sums over a family) was derived; the odd-k special c beyond k = 3 (c1 = -c2) and the matching of odd k with cohomological degrees were not studied; k = 9 and larger were not run.

## 5. Summary, limits, and what this does not say

Everything here is about character sums over F_p with the Weil bound as a known theorem; it says nothing about the Riemann Hypothesis for the integers, and every number is a finite computation at primes up to 73 (all c, k = 4), 17 (all c, k = 6), 101 (selected c, k = 6) and 311 to 677 (moments).

- M1 (CHECKED). k = 4, all c: the exceptional set at the 1/2 threshold is {c1+c2+c3 = 0, c3 != 0} (precision 0.994 for p <= 73, 0.998 for p >= 37; 2677 of 2694 such vectors have N/p^2 >= 1/2; the failures are at p <= 13 and two cases at p = 43, 59). It covers only 41% of all vectors with N/p^2 >= 1/2 up to p = 73 (49 to 50% for p >= 37); the rest are lower-order effects (M^4/p, c3 = 0, C(1)C(mu) at mu = tau(1), r*, and 191 unexplained rows at p <= 73), whose N/p^(3/2) stays within [-12.6, +15.7] with no clear growth. No exact condition on c describes them. Report I's closed form is confirmed (1148 cases).
- M2 (CHECKED + PROVED). k = 6: linear conditions for all 15 matchings listed; the brief's five give three one-parameter families at c1 = 1 (two need c1 = 0), all with c3 = 0 or c5 = 0. Only family B (c3 = 0, (c4,c5) = -(c1,c2)) shows N ~ (p-r0)^3 independently of M, and it is a sum of squares, N_6 = sum_s N_3(s)^2 (PROVED). Families A and D give N ~ (p-r0)^2 M^2 (data). 95% of excess-bad vectors at p = 11, 13, 17 lie on no matching subspace and are unexplained; at these p the next-order fluctuation (about 10 p^(5/2)) is as large as the plane term.
- M3 (CHECKED trends). A_1 -> 0 at about p^(-1/2) for generic c (and for all of k = 2..8, f1, f2, f4), and A_1 is near 1 on the k = 4 plane. A_2 hovers near 1 for random c and near 2 for reversal-symmetric (all-ones, palindromic) c, with deviation decaying about p^(-1/2) to p^(-3/4) (interpretation of the integer uses weights, not proved).
- M4 (PROVED for the degeneracy, CHECKED for sizes). Odd k: the path form has determinant 0 and a one-dimensional radical. For generic c, E = N - M^k/p is of size p^((k-1)/2) with no odd-even alternation (median |Z| about 0.9 at k = 5 and 0.66 at k = 7, against 0.69 at k = 6 and 0.66 at k = 8). For odd k there is no size gap that isolates exceptional c.

Not covered: k = 4 beyond p = 73 (all c) and k = 6 beyond p = 17 (all c); a mechanism for the unexplained excess-bad vectors at k = 6; f3 in M3; odd-k closed forms; k >= 9; extension fields F_(p^n); non-prime q. The run of `m4_odd.py` took about 10 minutes (at the cap); all other runs were shorter. CPU use was one process at a time at below-normal priority (numpy int64 operations, single threaded).
Circularity note: weights, purity and the Weil bound were used only to interpret the sizes (p^(3/2), p^(5/2), p^(-1/2), the integer limits of A_2) and never in a PROVED statement; the PROVED statements here are the matching conditions, the sum-of-squares identity for family B, Plancherel identities, the determinant and radical of the path form, and the c3 = 0 degeneration at k = 4.
