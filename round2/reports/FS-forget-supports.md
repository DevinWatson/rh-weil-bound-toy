# FS: forget-supports criterion (S-a, S-b), round 2

Scope: character sums over finite fields only (Weil 1948 is a theorem). Nothing here concerns RH for the integers.
Setting as in BRIEF.md: q odd, f squarefree of odd degree d>=3, f(0)!=0, chi quadratic, L = j_! L_chi(f), K = L^{boxtimes k} restricted to Y = {Q_c=0} in A^k, Q_c = sum_{a<k} c_a x_a x_{a+1}, all c_a != 0 (I assume this throughout). n := k-1 = dim Y.
No weights, purity or Weil bound are used in any proof below. Weights appear only in the interpretation remarks, each marked "(weights, interpretation only)".

## Verdict

1. S-a is correct, and I can state it exactly (section 1). It is an exact reformulation, not a reduction: (S_k) is equivalent to vanishing of explicit boundary groups B^m for m >= k plus surjectivity of one restriction map. The hypothesis "forget-supports is an isomorphism in all degrees" is far stronger than needed and is never true here (it fails along the S-b divisors); only degrees >= k matter.
2. S-b is correct as far as it goes (section 2): the closure is the divisor of type (1,...,1), the non-adjacent pair loci are boundary divisors lying in the closure, K extends there, and the extension is rank one with an explicit sheaf. But S-b is incomplete: the boundary also has strata with |I| >= 3 (all of codimension >= 2, singular points of the closure for |I| >= 4 and cones for |I| = 3), whose stalks I did not determine, and the cokernel in degree k is not a boundary invariant.
3. My theory does NOT reproduce the failure at k = 4, c_1+c_2+c_3 = 0 from generic-stratum data (section 4). Every boundary sheaf I can write down is insensitive to the vanishing of c_1+c_2+c_3. The failure lives in the cokernel of H^{k-1}(Y,K) -> B^{k-1} (or in unexamined lower strata), and I could not decide it. So "forget supports" does not decide (S_4) for c_1+c_2+c_3 != 0, and I give no proof of it.
4. The classical concentration theorems do not apply to the fibres h != 0 as stated, and their purity inputs are weight-based; see section 5 (mostly from memory).

## 1. S-a, exact statement and proof

Notation. Y_0 = Y ∩ U^k, U = A^1 minus roots of f. j_0: Y_0 -> Y. Ybar = any compactification of Y with j: Y -> Ybar open, boundary ∂ = Ybar \ Y, i: ∂ -> Ybar. Put B^m := H^m(∂, i^* Rj_* K) (∂ is proper, so this is H^m_c).

Lemma 1 (PROVED, given the cited facts). P := K[n] is perverse on Y (n = k-1), for every f (squarefree), every c with all c_a != 0.
Proof. Y is a hypersurface in A^k given by Q != 0, reduced (checked by hand: Q is linear in x_1 with coefficient c_1 x_2; a square factor g^2 with g free of x_1 would divide c_1x_2, so g ~ x_2, then x_2 | c_3x_3x_4+..., impossible for k>=4; for k=3, Q = x_2(c_1x_1+c_2x_3) is reduced; k=2 clear), hence Y is a local complete intersection of pure dimension n. The divisor V(g), g = prod f(x_i), contains no component of Y: Q is homogeneous, so every component of Y is a cone through 0, and a root a != 0 (here f(0) != 0 is used) gives hyperplanes x_i = a not through 0. So Y_0 is dense in Y, open, lci of pure dimension n, and j_0 is an affine open immersion (complement of the principal divisor V(g)|Y in the affine Y). On a lci of dimension n the constant sheaf, and any lisse sheaf, shifted by n is perverse (CITED: Kashiwara-Schapira, and in l-adic form Kiehl-Weissauer, "Weil conjectures, perverse sheaves and l-adic Fourier transform", Ch. III, lemma on lci; I did not open it, statement from memory; Goresky-MacPherson in the complex case). K = j_{0!}(rank one lisse), and j_{0!} is t-exact for affine quasi-finite j_0 (CITED: BBD 4.1.1 and 4.1.2, from memory, numbering not rechecked). Hence K[n] is perverse. QED.
Remark: the support condition alone is trivial (single sheaf in degree -n on dimension n); the content is the cosupport condition, which uses lci and affineness of j_0. The main session's one-line justification ("restriction of a perverse sheaf to a divisor with affine complement") is not by itself valid: i^*[-1] of a perverse sheaf is perverse only under extra conditions (non-characteristic, or the special form here). Correct conclusion, proof route differs. Note that the dualizing complex of Y is NOT a shifted constant sheaf in general (Y singular, e.g. a node), so one must not argue via omega_Y = Q[2n](n).

Theorem A (S-a, PROVED given Lemma 1 and Artin vanishing). Artin vanishing (CITED: SGA 4 XIV 3.1 for sheaves; BBD 4.1.1-4.1.2, Cor. 4.1.2 in perverse form: on an affine variety X over a separably closed field, H^j(X,P)=0 for j>0 and H^j_c(X,P)=0 for j<0 for P perverse; from memory). Applied to P = K[n] on the affine Y:
 (i) H^j_c(Y,K)=0 for j<n, and H^j(Y,K)=0 for j>n.
 (ii) With the triangle j_!K -> Rj_*K -> i_*i^*Rj_*K and Ybar proper, H^j_c(Y,K) = H^j(Ybar, j_!K):
   H^j_c(Y,K) ≅ B^{j-1} for j >= k+1,
   H^k_c(Y,K) = coker( H^{k-1}(Y,K) -> B^{k-1} ).
Hence (S_k) <=> [B^m = 0 for all m >= k] and [H^{k-1}(Y,K) -> B^{k-1} surjective]. Proof: long exact sequence ... H^{j-1}(Y,K) -> B^{j-1} -> H^j_c(Y,K) -> H^j(Y,K) -> B^j; for j >= k+1 both outer terms vanish by (i); for j = k only H^k(Y,K)=0 helps. QED.

Consequences. (a) A statement "iso in every degree" would force H^j(Y,K)=0 for j<n as well, i.e. j_!K = Rj_*K (clean); that is false whenever some boundary stalk of Rj_*K is nonzero (section 2). So S-a as worded in the brief is true but its hypothesis is never met; the useful form is the one above. (b) Necessary condition for (S_k): B^m = 0 for m >= k+1 and B^k = 0 (B^k injects? no: B^k receives H^k(Y,K)=0 and maps to H^{k+1}_c, so B^k ≅ H^{k+1}_c must vanish) -- i.e. all of B^m, m>=k, must vanish. This is checkable stratum by stratum in principle. (c) The degree-k group is the coker and needs H^{k-1}(Y,K), which is dual to a compactly supported group of the dual sheaf on the singular Y; it is not a boundary invariant. This is why the failure at k=4 does not show up in B alone (section 4).

## 2. Compactification and boundary strata

Homogeneous coordinates (X_i:Z_i), x_i = X_i/Z_i. Ybar := closure of Y in (P^1)^k.

PROVED. Q~ := sum_a c_a X_aX_{a+1} prod_{i != a,a+1} Z_i has multidegree (1,...,1) and Ybar = V(Q~) (Q~ is irreducible: restricted to Z_i=0 it is not identically zero for any single i, so V(Q~) has no component inside the boundary). Ybar is a divisor of type (1,...,1), dim k-1.
Strata S_I = {x_i = ∞ iff i in I}, I nonempty.
 - |I| >= 3: Q~ vanishes identically on S_I (each term omits all but two Z's). S_I ⊂ Ybar, dim k-|I|, codim >= 2 in Ybar.
 - |I| = 2: S_I ⊂ Ybar iff I = {a,b} is non-adjacent (each term contains Z_a or Z_b; for adjacent I the term c_a X_aX_{a+1} survives and is nonzero at ∞). Divisors of Ybar: D_ab, (k-1)(k-2)/2 of them.
 - |I| = 1, I={a}: S_a ∩ Ybar = D_a := {x_a=∞, l_a(x)=0}, l_a = c_{a-1}x_{a-1} + c_a x_{a+1} (a interior), l_1 = c_1x_2, l_k = c_{k-1}x_{k-1}. A divisor of Ybar, k of them.
 CHECKED (script compute/FS/strata.py, output compute/FS/strata_out.txt, symbolic in c, k=3..8): exactly the above containments, numbers of divisors (k-1)(k-2)/2 and k, adjacent pairs never contained, all |I|>=3 contained. Exact polynomial identity check, no floating point.

Local equation. In the chart u_i=1/x_i (i in I), E = sum_e c_e X_eX_{e+1} prod_{i in I \ e} u_i, X_i = 1 for i in I. For I = {a,b} non-adjacent: E = u_b l_a + u_a l_b + u_a u_b R (R polynomial in the finite coordinates). At points with l_a l_b != 0 Ybar is smooth, coordinates (u_a and the finite coordinates minus one), and u_b = u_a ψ with ψ = -l_b/l_a (up to units equal to 1 at u=0). (CHECKED by hand; k=4, a=1,b=3 also by direct computation in the text: E = c_1 x_2 u_3 + (c_2x_2 + c_3x_4) u_1.) Singular points of Ybar on D_13 at k=4: only x_2=x_4=0 (gradient computation).

Local sheaf. d odd gives chi(f(x)) = chi(u) · (unit with value 1 at u=0): L has monodromy -1 at ∞, so L extended by zero to P^1 equals Rj_* of itself; same at roots. Therefore:
 - Generic point of D_a: K ~ L_chi(u_a) ⊗ unramified; monodromy -1; (Rj_*K) stalk = 0. No correction. (PROVED, product structure in coordinates; at the points of D_a where other coordinates hit roots, K is j_! across those divisors, normal crossings except tangencies; not analysed.)
 - Generic point of D_ab: K ~ L_chi(u_a) ⊗ L_chi(u_a ψ) ⊗ rest = L_chi(ψ) ⊗ rest, unramified along D_ab (monodromy (-1)(-1) = +1). So Rj_*K has stalks R^0 = F_ab, R^1 = F_ab(-1), where
   F_ab = L_chi(-l_a l_b) ⊗ ⊗_{j != a,b} L(x_j) on the open part of D_ab ≅ affine space in the finite coordinates (chi(ψ)=chi(-l_al_b) since chi(l_a^{-2}) is trivial). PROVED (given local smoothness). This confirms S-b and gives the "explicit rank-one sheaf".
 - F_ab is geometrically nonconstant for every c (each variable x_j, j != a,b, carries chi(f(x_j)) possibly times chi of a polynomial of degree <= 2 in x_j; f times such a polynomial is never a square, since f is squarefree of degree >= 3). So H^2-top-degree coinvariants of F_ab on D_ab vanish and the top class of D_ab (H^{2(k-2)}_c) never appears. (PROVED for the open part.)
 Example k=3: D_13 is the curve x_1=x_3=∞, ψ = -c_2/c_1 constant, F = chi(-c_1c_2) L(x_2), giving H^1_c(L)(-1) in B^2 (degree k-1, harmless); the failure at c_1=-c_2 sits in the cokernel (the plane x_3=-(c_1/c_2)x_1 carries H^3_c = H^1_c(L) ⊗ Q(-1) when f(x)f(λx) is a square, matching Theorem A: H^3_c = coker(H^2(Y,K) -> B^2), non-surjective exactly when the plane sheaf is constant). Consistent with round one; derived by me from the product structure of Y at k=3 (Y = two planes), not by a boundary computation of the map.

Other strata (OPEN, not computed):
 - |I| = 3 pairwise non-adjacent: Ybar locally Σ l_i/u_i = -R, a quadric cone in (u_1,u_2,u_3); K ~ ⊗ L_chi(u_i), monodromy (-1)^3 around the axes; the corresponding global model is a Jacobi-sum, Σ_{a+b+c=s} chi(abc) = chi(-1) q for s != 0 (CHECKED mentally by the Gauss-sum identity G^3/G(chi^3) = G^2, G^2 = chi(-1)q; not run). Whether the local stalk of Rj_*K at S_I is nonzero I did not determine (HINT: plausibly nonzero, rank 1, because the local tube is a Kummer-twisted affine quadric whose Jacobi-type compactly supported cohomology is one-dimensional).
 - |I| = 2 non-adjacent plus other coordinates at roots / l_a=0 / l_b=0: tangential strata (cf. H's tangent strata), not analysed.
 - The vertex (x=0) and the origin-related loci: Q nondegenerate (k even) so Y has an isolated singular point at 0, which lies in Y (interior), outside the boundary; it matters only through H^{k-1}(Y,K).
 So the answer to "every other such stratum" is: the divisors D_ab are the only boundary divisors with a nonzero generic stalk of Rj_*K; every other boundary divisor (the D_a) has zero generic stalk; strata of codimension >= 2 (|I|>=3, and special loci inside D_ab) are undetermined.

## 3. Correction groups (what can and cannot be said)

Stratification spectral sequence: B^m is built from H^*_c(S, Rj_*K|_S) over strata S. The open strata D_ab° contribute H^{m}_c(D_ab°, F_ab) (via R^0) and H^{m-1}_c(D_ab°, F_ab)(-1) (via R^1). Since D_ab° is affine of dimension k-2 and F_ab is nonconstant, H^j_c(D_ab°,F_ab) lives in degrees k-2 < j <= 2(k-2) - 1, and in particular H^{2(k-2)}_c=0 (PROVED above). (Weights, interpretation only: the middle part is not pure; no use made.)
At k=4: the three divisors D_13, D_24 (F = chi(-c_1 x_2(c_2x_2+c_3x_4) f(x_2)f(x_4)) and its mirror) and D_14 (F = L_chi(x f(x))_{x_2} ⊠ L_chi(x f(x))_{x_3}, up to the constant chi(-c_1c_3)) give
 B^m ⊃ H^m_c(D,F) ⊕ H^{m-1}_c(D,F)(-1).
For D_14: H^*_c is H^1_c ⊗ H^1_c in degree 2 only (PROVED: product of two nonconstant rank-one sheaves on the line, Kunneth), contributing to B^2 and B^3 only: harmless (degree <= k-1 = 3).
For D_13, D_24: degrees 3 only (affine surface, nonconstant => H^2_c=H^4_c=0, H^3_c !=0 generically since the Euler characteristic is nonzero -- not computed exactly). They contribute H^3_c(F)(-1) to B^4. But round one proved H^5_c = H^6_c = 0, i.e. B^4 = B^5 = 0. Therefore those classes MUST be cancelled by lower strata (curves and points in D_13, D_24 and the |I|>=3 strata). This is a real constraint on the unfinished stalk computations and I did not close it (OPEN). If the lower-stratum stalks were all zero, the theory would contradict round one; so at least one of them is nonzero. (PROVED as a deduction from round one's H^5_c=H^6_c=0 plus Theorem A and the generic stalk computation, provided H^3_c(D_13°,F)!=0 and no cancellation within the open parts of D_13.)

## 4. k = 4 and the failure at c_1+c_2+c_3 = 0

Plain statement of what happens: it does not come out.
 - The only place the quantity c_1+c_2+c_3 appears in the boundary data is the restriction of ψ = -l_3/l_1 to the diagonal Δ = {x_2=x_4} ⊂ D_13: ψ|_Δ = -(c_2+c_3)/c_1, equal to 1 iff c_1+c_2+c_3=0. But F_13|_Δ = chi(ψ|_Δ) t(x_2)^2 is geometrically constant on Δ for EVERY c (ψ|_Δ is a constant), so nothing changes geometrically at c_1+c_2+c_3=0; only the arithmetic sign chi(-(c_2+c_3)c_1) changes. The same holds for D_24, and for D_14 on its diagonal x_2=x_3 (always constant, for all c). So generic-stratum boundary data are blind to the bad locus. CHECKED by symbolic evaluation of ψ.
 - The plane P = {x_1=x_3, x_2=x_4} lies in Y iff c_1+c_2+c_3=0; there K|_P is constant on U^2 and carries H^4_c(P)=Q(-2) (round one). In Theorem A terms the failure is H^4_c(Y,K) = coker(H^3(Y,K) -> B^3) != 0. B^3 itself is nonzero for every c (it contains H^1_c ⊗H^1_c(-1) from D_14 and H^3_c(D_13,F)(R^0), H^2_c(D_13,F)(-1)=0 ...), so B^3 does not see the dichotomy; the cokernel does, and it needs H^3(Y,K) with Y singular at 0. I could not compute it.
 So: my theory REPRODUCES neither the failure nor its absence for generic c at k=4 beyond what round one proved. For generic c it gives necessary conditions only (B^m=0, m>=4), which are consistent with H^5_c=H^6_c=0 only if lower strata cancel the D_13/D_24 classes; not verified. I state this plainly.
 What survives: a clean reduction of (S_4) for c_1+c_2+c_3 != 0 to (a) B^4=0 (equivalently H^5_c=0, already proved by round one) and (b) surjectivity of H^3(Y,K) -> B^3. (b) is open, and no weight-free argument is known to me.
 Exact check of data against the other round-one claim: not repeated; no point counts were run for this report.

## 5. Comparison with concentration theorems

What I could verify: only that Deligne's "Applications de la formule des traces aux sommes trigonometriques" is exposé "Sommes trig." in SGA 4 1/2 (Springer LNM 569, 1977, p. 168 ff. of the volume) and that a later paper cites its Theoreme 8.4 for a degree-d polynomial prime to p with nonsingular projective leading hypersurface, giving the bound (d-1)^n q^{n/2} (search result summary, arXiv alg-geom/9702006; I did not open the source). I could not confirm in the sources the exact vanishing statement; everything below on the proofs is from memory and UNVERIFIED.
 - Deligne, Sommes trig., 8.4 (memory): F polynomial on A^n of degree d prime to p whose top-degree form defines a smooth hypersurface in P^{n-1}; then H^i_c(A^n_bar, L_psi(F)) = 0 for i != n, dim H^n_c = (d-1)^n, pure of weight n. Concentration via the structure of L_psi at infinity (the fibre cohomology/vanishing cycles computation at infinity, local acyclicity) and the Euler characteristic; purity of H^n_c is the weight part (Weil II/Weil I). Hypotheses fail here: our sheaf is L_psi(hQ) ⊗ ⊗ L_chi(f(x_i)), with non-trivial tame twists, and Q (degree 2) has smooth quadric leading form for k even, but the twist makes it a twisted sum.
 - Katz, Sommes exponentielles, Asterisque 79 (1980): sums over the torus with multiplicative characters and L_psi, Euler characteristic (Deligne-Katz), concentration for nondegenerate hypersurface sums. Needs monomial characters (Kummer on G_m^n) in coordinates, not Kummer sheaves of a non-monomial f(x). (memory)
 - Adolphson-Sperber (Ann. of Math. 130 (1989), twisted version Crelle 443 (1993)); Denef-Loeser (Invent. 106 (1991)): nondegeneracy relative to the Newton polyhedron of F (not of f(x_i) twists); concentration in degree n and weights from nondegeneracy; concentration proved via p-adic cohomology (Adolphson-Sperber, which is weight-free for the dimension count) and via toric compactification for Denef-Loeser, purity/weights via Weil II. (memory)
 Do they apply to the fibres h != 0? Not as stated: the fibre sum S(h) = Σ ∏chi(f(x_i)) psi(hQ(x)) has the twist L_chi(f(x_i)) ramified at the finite roots of f, which are not toric. Katz-Laumon style iteration (k-1 Fourier transforms, each followed by multiplication by a character sheaf) is the tool that does apply, and it is the sheaf form of iterated Parseval used in round one; its basic exactness statements (Fourier transform preserves perverse sheaves up to shift; BBD / Katz-Laumon / Kiehl-Weissauer Ch. III, memory) are weight-free, but stating concentration of the end result still requires an irreducibility/monodromy input, and every proof I know of that uses weights (Katz-Laumon, Weil II) is what would make a proof of (S_k) circular.
 Are their concentration proofs weight-free? My memory: the DIMENSION/concentration part (Deligne's Euler characteristic and the vanishing H^i_c = 0, i != n, for nondegenerate L_psi sums) is proved from local acyclicity and the Euler characteristic formula without Weil II; purity is the weight part. This is UNVERIFIED and I could not open SGA 4 1/2. In the regime where weights are unavailable the relevant fact is the nondegeneracy hypothesis, which the twisted sum with non-monomial f does not satisfy.
 Relation to the forget-supports criterion: for pure L_psi sums nondegeneracy at infinity makes L_psi "clean" at infinity (H^i_c -> H^i iso), which is the analogue of Theorem A with B = 0. In our problem B != 0 on the divisors D_ab (monodromy (-1)(-1)=1 makes K extend), i.e. the problem is not clean at infinity; so the standard hypothesis is violated exactly where S-b says.
