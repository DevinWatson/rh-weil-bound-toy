### 2.1 The linear conditions (PROVED, elementary)
Take a perfect matching sigma of {1..6} and the subspace x_i = x_sigma(i), dimension 3, coordinates y_1, y_2, y_3 (one per pair). The term c_i x_i x_{i+1} becomes c_i y_a y_b, where a and b are the pairs of i and i+1 (a monomial y_a^2 if i and i+1 are matched to each other). Q_c vanishes identically on the subspace iff, for each pair of pairs {a,b}, the sum of the c_i over the edges (i,i+1) joining a and b is 0, and, for an edge inside a pair, its c_i is 0. On the subspace the weight is the product of t(y_a)^2, so the sum over the subspace is (p-r0)^3. All 15 matchings with their conditions are in `m2_matchings.txt`. The five matchings with no adjacent pair (the brief's set) and their conditions:
- 1-3, 2-5, 4-6: c1+c2 = 0, c3 = 0, c4+c5 = 0
- 1-4, 2-5, 3-6: c1+c4 = 0, c3 = 0, c2+c5 = 0
- 1-4, 2-6, 3-5: c1 = 0, c3+c4 = 0, c2+c5 = 0
- 1-5, 2-4, 3-6: c1+c4 = 0, c5 = 0, c2+c3 = 0
- 1-6, 2-4, 3-5: c1 = 0, c5 = 0, c2+c3+c4 = 0
Two of the five force c1 = 0. Under the normalisation c1 = 1 they are empty (for c1 = 0, x1 does not occur in Q and N = M N_5). The other three are the one-parameter families A: c = (1,-1,0,u,-u); B: c = (1,u,0,-1,-u); D: c = (1,u,-u,-1,0). Their union has 3p-2 vectors (CHECKED: 31, 37, 49 vectors for p = 11, 13, 17 by enumeration over all p^4 vectors, `m2_predicted.tsv`, and the families generated from these formulas lie in the enumerated set). Every one of the three has c3 = 0 or c5 = 0 (visible from the lists): the brief's non-adjacent matchings never give a vector with all c_i nonzero at k = 6. The union over all 15 matchings (c1 = 1) has 20, 30, 50, 60, 80 vectors at p = 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 (`m2_summary.txt`, column 'any').

### 2.2 What the data say (CHECKED)
1. Raw list N_6/p^3 >= 1/2 (exact, column `2N>=p^3` in the `m2_exhaustive*.tsv` files). The count is dominated by the main term M^6/p of 'qN = M^k + R' whenever |M| is near 2 sqrt(p): then M^6/p alone exceeds p^3/2 and nearly every c qualifies (f3 p=7: 2401 of 2401; f3 p=17: 75998 of 83521; f2 p=7: 1173 of 2401). So the raw bad set is a property of (f, p), not an exceptional set of c. I therefore also report the excess E = N - M^6/p (column `2E>=p^3`).
2. Explained and unexplained (exact counts, `m2_summary.txt`; 'any' = predicted by one of the 15 matchings). f1 p=17: 759 raw-bad (= excess-bad), 19 on the brief's families, 726 on no matching subspace. f1 p=13: 368 raw-bad and 360 excess-bad, 8 on the families, 348 unexplained. f1 p=11: 259, 245, 13, 226. f4 p=17: 2113, 1223, 47, 1147. f4 p=13: 39, 39, 7, 26. f3 p=13: 9, 9, 2, 7. f2 p=13: 1583, 658, 31, 606. Summed over all exhaustive tables with p = 11, 13, 17 (`m2b_unexplained.txt`): 7352 of the 7723 excess-bad vectors lie on no matching subspace (95.2% unexplained). Conversely, of the vectors on the brief's families at p = 11, 13, 17 only some are excess-bad: f1 13 of 31, 8 of 37, 19 of 49; f3 23 of 31, 2 of 37, 41 of 49; f4 21 of 31, 7 of 37, 47 of 49; f2 16 of 31, 31 of 37.
3. The three families behave differently (CHECKED trend; mechanism HINT). `m2d_large_p.tsv` and `m2e_family_ratio.txt` (f1 and f3, p = 23..101, all 3p-2 vectors). Family B has N/(p-r0)^3 between 0.9 and 1.06 for every (f,p) with |M| small (f1 p=29 1.016, p=41 0.965, p=67 0.905, p=83 0.937; f3 p=29 0.988, p=41 0.908, p=67 0.993, p=101 0.962) and 1.4 to 2.6 when |M| is large (the main term). Families A and D have N close to 0 when M is 0 or small (f1 p=29: -0.011 and 0.000; f1 p=83: 0.026 and -0.002) and N/((p-r0)^2 M^2) near 1 (0.9 to 1.4) when |M| >= 9. So only family B carries a p^3 term independent of M. For A and D, c3 = 0 or c5 = 0 and Y contains a 4-dimensional component with two free coordinates whose weights sum to M each; I did not prove that this accounts for the data.
4. Family B is a sum of squares (PROVED): for c = (c1, c2, 0, -c1, -c2), Q_c(x) = Q_a(x1,x2,x3) - Q_a(x4,x5,x6) with Q_a = c1 x1 x2 + c2 x2 x3. Hence N_6(c) = sum over s of N_3(c1,c2; s)^2, which is at least M^6/p by Cauchy-Schwarz and sum_s N_3(s) = M^3. By Plancherel (algebra), N_6(c) = M^6/p + ((p-1)/p) times the mean over h != 0 of |S_3(h)|^2. So N_6(B)/p^3 = M^6/p^4 + (1 - 1/p) A_2(k=3), with A_2 as in section 3. In general sum_s N_k(c; s)^2 = N_{2k}(c, 0, -c), so the A_2 values of section 3 are values of N_{2k} on the doubled family.
5. The plane signal is not resolved at p <= 17. On the brief's families, (E - (p-r0)^3)/p^(5/2) has median between -3.4 and 3.5 but ranges from -6.1 to +14.6 (`m2c_plane_signal.txt`): deviations of 5 to 15 times p^(5/2) against a plane term of 3 to 4 times p^(5/2) at p = 11..17. A fluctuation of size 10 p^(5/2) only drops below p^3/2 near p = 400 (extrapolation of float statistics, HINT).
6. No exact condition describes the unexplained excess-bad vectors. Simple predicates (a coordinate c_i = 0, vanishing sums of consecutive blocks) show weak enrichment over the baseline (`m2b_unexplained.txt`, `m2f_baseline.txt`; for example f1 p=17: some c_i = 0 in 33% of the unexplained against 21.5% of all c; f3 p=17: 88% against 21.5%). Their size is of the next order: over all c on no matching subspace, N/p^(5/2) is at most 12.0 (float, `m2b_unexplained.txt`), not of order p^3. Unexplained means: 7352 vectors at p = 11, 13, 17 for which I have no mechanism.
7. At larger p the matching subspaces separate better: for random c at p = 41..101 (60 per case) N/p^3 reaches 1/2 only where |M| is large (f1 p=53, M = -12: 17 of 60; f3 p=41: 1 of 60 at 0.590), while family B always does (`m2d_large_p_summary.txt`).

Finding for k = 6: the only exact bad-c mechanism confirmed by data and proved to exist is family B, where N_6 is a sum of squares. The other brief matchings (families A and D) do not give N of order p^3 independently of f. Of the excess-bad vectors at p <= 17, 95% are unexplained by any matching subspace.
