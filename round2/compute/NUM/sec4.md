
## 4. M4: odd k (k = 5, 7) versus even k

Scripts: `m4_odd.py` (writes `m4_odd_summary.txt`, `m4a_random.tsv`, `m4b_k5_exhaustive.tsv`; this run took about 10 minutes, at the cap), `m4c_robust.py` -> `m4c_robust.txt`, `m4d_outliers.py` -> `m4d_outliers.txt`.

1. The degeneracy (PROVED over Q by sympy, `m4_odd_summary.txt`, and elementary): the Gram matrix of Q_c has determinant c1^2 c3^2 (k=4), -c1^2 c3^2 c5^2 (k=6) for even k, and 0 for odd k (k = 3, 5, 7, 9 checked symbolically; the matrix is tridiagonal with zero diagonal, so odd size gives determinant 0). For odd k the radical is one-dimensional, with r_even = 0, and (sympy, normalised r_k = 1) is: k=3: (-c2/c1, 0, 1); k=5: (c2 c4/(c1 c3), 0, -c4/c3, 0, 1); k=7: (-c2 c4 c6/(c1 c3 c5), 0, c4 c6/(c3 c5), 0, -c6/c5, 0, 1). So Q_c(x + lambda r) = Q_c(x): Y is a cylinder in the direction r, and the sum over the lines x + lambda r of the weights prod t(x_i + lambda r_i) (for odd i) times the fixed weights of the even coordinates is a character sum in lambda of a polynomial of degree (k+1)/2 times f. This is the structural difference from even k; its size for generic c is what the next items measure.
2. Size of N_k(0) for generic c (CHECKED, `m4a_random.tsv`): for f1, f2, f4, primes 61..311, 10 random c per (poly, prime) (entries uniform in F_p^*, seeds `m4a-<poly>-<k>-<p>-<j>`), k = 3..8, 1400 samples each. With E = N_k(0) - M^k/p (exact) and Z = E / p^((k-1)/2):
   - median |Z|: 0.478 (k=3), 1.172 (4), 0.905 (5), 0.689 (6), 0.661 (7), 0.658 (8);
   - RMS Z for p >= 150: 1.73, 1.93, 1.45, 1.03, 0.99, 0.99; RMS Z excluding the top 1% of |Z|: 1.45, 1.62, 1.37, 1.00, 0.96, 0.94 (`m4c_robust.txt`);
   - fraction of Z > 0: 0.543, 0.551, 0.514, 0.483, 0.502, 0.498.
   So E has size p^((k-1)/2) for generic c at every k from 3 to 8, with no alternation between odd and even k: k = 5 and k = 7 sit on the smooth curve through k = 4, 6, 8 (the spread decreases with k: 1.9 at k = 4, 1.45 at k = 5, 1.03 at k = 6, then 0.99 at k = 7 and 8). The k = 6, 7, 8 values (median about 0.66, RMS about 1.0) are what a standard Gaussian would give (median 0.674); that is a descriptive remark (HINT), not a claim about the distribution.
   - Mean Z is within 0.03 of 0 at k = 5..8; the k = 3, 4 means (+0.14, +0.28) are driven by the rare exceptional c (next item).
3. Exceptional c at small k (CHECKED against the regenerated c, `m4d_outliers.txt`): in the random samples, the vanishing blocks c1+c2 = 0 (k=3) and c1+c2+c3 = 0 (k=4) appear in 6 of the 53 outliers |Z| > 4 at k = 3 and in 8 of the 29 at k = 4, against baseline frequencies 0.004 and 0.021 over all samples. The other outliers at k = 3, 4 are the heavy tail of the closed forms (k = 3: N_3 = t0 M^2 + (M - t0) C(r), product of two O(sqrt p) quantities divided by p). For k = 5..8 the outliers (|Z| > 3 at k = 5, > 2.5 at k = 6, 7, 8) are not explained by any vanishing block sum of length >= 2: 4 of 44 at k = 5 (baseline 0.034), 5 of 20 at k = 6 (0.066), 3 of 14 at k = 7 (0.090), 2 of 14 at k = 8 (0.136). No condition found. The family-B vectors of M2 (c3 = 0) cannot appear in these samples because entries were drawn nonzero.
4. What exceptional means for odd k (CHECKED + reasoning): for even k the matching subspaces have dimension k/2 and contribute (p-r0)^(k/2), larger than the generic size p^((k-1)/2) by a factor p^(1/2), so a threshold N/p^(k/2) >= 1/2 isolates special c (section 1 and 2). For odd k the analogous subspaces have dimension (k-1)/2 (one coordinate forced to 0 and a matching of the others) and contribute of order p^((k-1)/2), the same order as generic N_k; there is no gap, and the corresponding threshold N/p^((k-1)/2) >= 1/2 is met by 16% to 34% of all c (more where |M| is near 2 sqrt p): k = 5, c = (1,c2,c3,c4) exhaustive at p = 11, 13, 17 (`m4b_k5_exhaustive.tsv`): fraction with 2N >= p^2 is 403/1331 and 552/2197 and 1443/4913 for f1 at p = 11, 13, 17; 447/1331, 539/2197 for f2; 356/2197 for f3 at 13; 488/2197 for f4 at 13 (with larger fractions where |M| is near 2 sqrt p, e.g. f3 p = 17: 4905 of 4913). My attempt to explain these by subspaces of the form (coordinate = 0) + (matching) was not informative: the union of the 15 subspaces contains 38%, 33% and 26% of all c (503 of 1331 at p=11, 723 of 2197 at 13, 1283 of 4913 at 17), so I make no claim from that test. Conclusion: at odd k the route has no clean separation of a 'bad set' from the bulk at the scale of the main term, so (S_k)-type statements cannot be tested by counting bad c as at even k; one has to look at E = N - M^k/p instead (item 2), and for odd k the correct reference size is p^((k-1)/2), not p^(k/2).
5. Caveat on item 2 (CHECKED): the raw N_k(0), unlike E, is dominated by the main term M^k/p at k = 7, 8 for these p: RMS of M^k/p^((k+1)/2) over the same (poly,p) pairs is 0.16, 0.25, 0.42, 0.72, 1.26, 2.2 for k = 3..8 (`m4c_robust.txt`), so the RMS of N/p^((k-1)/2) is 1.62 (k=7) and 2.43 (k=8) while that of E is 0.99. 'The sums N_k have size about p^((k-1)/2)' is true for the excess E, and for N itself only while M^k/p is small.
6. Not done: no odd-k analogue of the closed form of report I (N_5, N_7 as sums over a family) was derived; the odd-k special c beyond k = 3 (c1 = -c2) and the matching of odd k with cohomological degrees were not studied; k = 9 and larger were not run.
