## 1. Laumon 1987, chapter 2 (OPENED, OCR text)

1. Definition (2.4.2.3), l.1261-1272 (CITED, OPENED). The local Fourier transforms F^(0,inf'), F^(inf,0'), F^(inf,inf') are defined as R^1 Phi (vanishing cycles) of pr^*(V_!) tensor L(x x') (or L(1/(pi pi'))), from I-modules at one point to I-modules at another. Preparation: Lemma 2.4.2.1 and Prop. 2.4.2.2 (concentration in degree 1).
2. Proposition 2.3.3.1 (l.1168-1179) (CITED, OPENED). In the dump it is a PROPOSITION, not a "Theorem"; the brief's "stationary phase theorem 2.3.3.1" is right in content, wrong in label. Statement: for the nearby cycles at infinity of pr^*(K) tensor L(x x') [1]: (i) the restriction to U x eta_inf' is zero; (ii) the R^i Phi vanish for i != -1; (iii) a functorial isomorphism for K in Perv(A): K'_{eta_inf'} = (+)_{s in S} Ind(R^{-1}Phi_{s,...}) (+) R^{-1}Phi_{inf,inf'}(...) as I_inf'-modules (induction subscripts garbled). This is the stationary-phase decomposition of FT(K) at infinity into contributions of the finite singular points of K and of K at infinity. Laumon's introduction (l.113-116): "l'analogue algebrique du calcul des developpements asymptotiques d'integrales oscillantes, base sur le principe de la phase stationnaire".
3. Proposition 2.3.1.1 (l.1071-1081) (CITED, OPENED). K perverse on A, K' = F(K), F = H^{-1}(K)|U, F_inf the I_inf-module: (i) r(K') = sum_s deg(s) a_s(K) + [a term from the slope >1 part of F_inf]; (ii) for s' != 0' the drop of the stalk rank of K' at s' below the generic rank is r((F_inf)_1) - r(((F_inf)_1 tensor L_psi(x s'))_{..}), with (F_inf)_1 the slope-1 part; (iii) the same at 0' with the slope <1 part. Subscripts garbled; the dependence is clear: the rank at s' != 0' depends only on the slope-1 part of F_inf and on s'.
4. Corollaire 2.3.1.2 (l.1100-1105) (CITED, OPENED): "L'ouvert V ne depend de K que par l'intermediaire du I_inf'-module (F_inf)_{[1]}"; s' != 0' is in V (K' lisse at s') iff a rank condition on (F_inf)_1 tensor L(x s') holds; 0' is in V iff the slope <1 part of F_inf vanishes. This is the statement locating the finite singular points of FT(K) in terms of K at infinity. Corollaire 2.3.1.3 (l.1109-1119): (i) F_inf nonzero and purely of slopes <1 => U' = A' - {0'}; (ii) F_inf purely of slopes >1 => V = A'.
5. Prop. 2.3.2.1, Cor. 2.3.2.3 (l.1136-1156) (OPENED): vanishing cycles of K' at s' != 0' are nearby cycles of pr^*(K) tensor L at the points over s'; rank formula as in 3. Remarque 2.3.2.4: a special case of Deligne's Swan-conductor semicontinuity [Lau 3] (5.1.1) (item 4 of this report). Remarque 2.3.2.2: special case of the Artin-Gabber t-exactness of Psi, Phi (BBD 4.4).
6. Theoreme 2.4.3 (l.1285-1323) (CITED, OPENED, formulas partly garbled). (i)a F^(0,inf') exact; (i)b r(F^(0,inf')(V)) = r(V)+Sw(V) and Swan(F(V)) = Sw(V); (i)c equivalence of abelian categories with quasi-inverse a^*F^(inf,0')(-)(1); (ii) F^(inf,0') is zero on slopes in ]1,inf[ and an equivalence on slopes in [0,1[; (iii) F^(inf,inf') is zero on slopes in [0,1] and an equivalence on ]1,inf[, with r(F^(inf,inf')(V)) = Sw(V) - r(V). The rank and Swan formulas read from the OCR agree with the slope maps s/(1+s), s/(1-s), s/(s-1) (HINT: derived from them); I did NOT check the garbled symbols character by character.
7. Proposition 2.5.3.1 (l.1360-1375) (CITED, OPENED). (i) Trivial module: F^(0,inf')(Q_l)=Q_l, F^(inf,0')(Q_l)=Q_l(-1), F^(inf,inf')(Q_l)=0. (ii) For a nontrivial Kummer sheaf L_chi: F^(0,inf')(V_chi) = V'_chi tensor G(chi,psi), F^(inf,0')(V_chi) = (Kummer module of chi^{-1}) tensor G(chi,psi), F^(inf,inf')(V_chi) = 0, with G(chi,psi) = H^1_c(G_m, L_chi tensor L_psi) an unramified (constant) module. ## 2. Is FT an equivalence of perverse sheaves; are simples preserved?

- CITED, OPENED. Laumon Theoreme 1.3.2.3 (volume p. 147): "F transforme les Q_l-faisceaux pervers sur E en Q_l-faisceaux pervers sur E'. Le foncteur F: Perv(E) -> Perv(E') est une equivalence de categories abeliennes de quasi-inverse a^*F'(-)(r)." Proof: pr smooth of relative dimension r so pr^*[r] is t-exact (BBD 4.2.5); pr' affine so one of pr'_!, pr'_* is left t-exact (BBD 4.1.2) and the other right t-exact (BBD 4.1.1); the forget-supports isomorphism 1.3.1.1 (= Katz-Laumon 2.1.3) identifies them; the second assertion from involutivity 1.2.2.1, 1.2.2.3. No weights (item 1.9).
- CITED, OPENED. Corollaire 1.3.2.4: "F transforme les Q_l-faisceaux pervers simples sur E en Q_l-faisceaux pervers simples sur E'." (An equivalence of abelian categories preserves simples anyway.)
- CITED, OPENED. Theoreme 1.4.2.1 (rank one over Spec k, volume pp. 148-149): simple perverse sheaves on A are of three types: (T1) i_*F, punctual; (T2) pr_*(L_psi(x,j') tensor pr'^*F')[1], the L_psi(a x)-type; (T3) j_*F[1] with F lisse irreducible on a dense open U and no subquotient of F|U (bar) isomorphic to L_psi(x,a')|U for any a'. "(i) F echange les types (T1) et (T2); (ii) F echange les types (T3) et (T3')." Laumon adds that from j_*F[1] of type T3 one gets the maximal open U' and an irreducible lisse F' on U', and that "la determination complete de F' reste pour l'instant inaccessible": only U', rank F' and, "dans une certaine mesure", local monodromies at the missing points are accessible (section 2.3).
- So Claim B step 2 is exactly 1.4.2.1(ii) plus excluding types T1, T2; the brief's reason (ramified at the roots, hence neither punctual nor L_psi(a x)[1]) does exclude both. OPENED.
- Katz-Laumon 2.1.5: only as quoted by Laumon (after 1.3.2.1: "Pour L = Q_l,S, (1.3.2.1) n'est autre que [Ka-La] (2.1.5)(ii)": FT commutes with Verdier duality up to a twist). NOT OPENED as a numbered statement of Katz-Laumon here.

## 3. Novelty: mechanisms and comparison

Sources: Laumon ch. 4 (OPENED, OCR, l.2944-3129); Katz, "L-functions and monodromy: four lectures on Weil II", Adv. Math. 160 (2001) 81-132, read in the Arizona Winter School version https://swc-math.github.io/notes/files/00KatzN.pdf (OPENED, whole text); Venkatesh, Stanford Weil II seminar note on the l-adic Fourier transform, http://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/Weil2seminar/Notes/L23.pdf (OPENED); Kiehl-Weissauer: NOT OPENED.

### 3.1 Laumon ch. 4, ten lines (CITED, OPENED)
1. Target (Cor. 4.3.1.1, l.3028-3032): F lisse irreducible, pure of weight w, geometrically non-constant, unramified at infinity on the affine line: every eigenvalue on H^1_c has modulus <= q^((w+1)/2). Prop. 4.3.2.1 shows this implies Deligne's theorem 4.1.3.
2. Proof via F' with FT(j_!F[1]) = j'_!F'[1], F' irreducible lisse on A'-{0'} (1.4.2.1, 2.3.1.3(i)): the generic stalk of F' near 0' contains H^1_c(A, j_!F) (exact sequence l.3089-3091, from 2.3.2, 2.5.3.1(i), proper base change).
3. If F' is pure of weight w', Deligne's lemma 4.2.2.2 (Weil II 1.8.4) gives w' = w+1, which gives the bound.
4. Purity of F' comes from Deligne's criterion 4.2.1.3 (Weil II 1.5.1: irreducible lisse constituents of an iota-real lisse sheaf on a curve are pure): F' is a constituent of G' = FT(j_!(F tensor Fbar(-w))), and one must show G' is real.
5. Reality of G' comes from the L-function product formula, det(1 - t Frob_x', G') = product over x of factors built from F tensor Fbar (l.3118-3128), real by 4.2.2.3.
6. Plancherel/Parseval: nowhere in chapter 4; grep of the whole dump finds neither word. The Fourier transform enters as a family (the stalk at y of FT(K) is H_c(A, K tensor L_psi(yx))) with involutivity.
7. Positivity/sign: only inside the cited criterion 4.2.1.3 (not reproved in the text) and in "iota-real".
8. Limit over tensor powers: not in Laumon's text; it sits inside the cited criterion (HINT, from 3.2).
9. Field extensions: only "after finite extension" (4.3.2.1) to make infinity rational, and the product over all closed points.
10. Induction on weights (weight of F' from weight of F); the proof is not weight-free.

### 3.2 Katz, four lectures, ten lines (CITED, OPENED)
1. Idea (Lecture I): "put the particular L-function ... into a family of L-functions which has big monodromy, then to apply the Rankin squaring method to this family. So we end up with a proof of Weil II in the style of Weil I." Fourier transform enters through "an elementary instance of the involutivity [Lau-TF, 1.2.2.1]".
2. Auxiliary lisse sheaf T on A^2 with stalk H^1_c(A^1, F tensor L_psi(f(T) + aT + bT^2)).
3. Positivity: T tensor Tbar(1) has trace function (1/q_E)|iota(Trace(Frob|T))|^2 >= 0 ("Positive Trace Corollary").
4. Rankin step (Lecture III): L-function of (T tensor Tbar)^{tensor a} on a curve C_d has coefficients >= 0 ("Positive Coefficient Lemma"); H^2_c is the coinvariants of (V tensor V^vee)^{tensor a}, whose Frobenius eigenvalues are q_k times roots of unity (uses that T|C_d has the same G_geom as T); hence the L-function is holomorphic in |T| < 1/q_k.
5. One closed point's single term is coefficientwise dominated by the whole series, so every eigenvalue of Frob on (T tensor Tbar)^{tensor a} has modulus <= q_E; hence |alpha|^a <= q_E for every eigenvalue alpha of Frob on T tensor Tbar; "Letting a -> infinity" gives |alpha| <= 1 and, with trivial determinant, equality: purity of T.
6. Tensor powers and the limit enter exactly in step 5: exponent a, constant independent of a.
7. Orthogonality (Parseval-type) enters only in Lecture IV Step 5, the monodromy theorem: the cohomological form of sum_{a,b} psi(a l + b m) = q^2 [(l,m)=0] is H^*_c(A^1, L_psi(l a + m b)) = 0 for (l,m) != 0, applied to the fourth moment with phase a(x+y-z-w) + b(x^2+y^2-z^2-w^2); the surviving locus Z is two (odd characteristic) or three (characteristic 2) planes, giving dim H^4_c(A^2, T tensor T tensor Tbar tensor Tbar) = 2 (resp. 3) by Kunneth and Schur. Weights appear only in an "Interlude" as a heuristic.
8. Irreducibility (Step 1): T|_{b=0} is the naive Fourier transform of F tensor L_psi(f); "NFT induces an autoequivalence of the abelian category of those lisse sheaves on A^1 all of whose infinity-breaks are > 1" (Katz [Ka-ESDE 7.8], "a consequence of Laumon's general theory"), hence preserves geometric irreducibility. Same Laumon equivalence as Claim B step 2.
9. Group theory: moment criterion [Ka-MCG]: irreducible G with finite determinant and M_4 = 2 (resp. 3) is finite or contains SL (resp. SO/Sp).
10. Input: F pure of weight 0 on the line; purity of T is derived. (My reading; earlier lemmas of lectures I-II not all reread.)

### 3.3 The common engine (Venkatesh's note, OPENED)
"to bound a single value of a function, put that function in a family and raise it to a higher power. This idea was used again by Rankin" (p. 3). Method of families: g >= 0, g(x) <= sum g, so max|alpha| <= max|beta| where beta runs over eigenvalues on H^2_c (coinvariants) "because the expression is positive" (p. 5); the family is g = FT(f) and the value to bound is its value at 0 (p. 5-6). Kloosterman's 4th-moment computation (sum_a |K(a)|^4 via sum_a psi(a(x+y-z-w)) = p [x+y=z+w]) gives |K(a)| <= c p^(3/4).

### 3.5 Comparison with the route of the brief
Route: q N = M^k + R (point count on the path quadric Y = k-th power of the single sum plus h-modes); Parseval over h, k-1 times, giving fibres P_k; one-degree concentration (Claim B); eigenvalues on constant quotients bounded within a fibre (Claim A); k -> infinity.
- Same engine as Kloosterman-Rankin-Laumon-Katz (HINT, from the sources): one eigenvalue is bounded by a k-th power moment of a family (exponent k here, a there); the limit removes a constant; dangerous eigenvalues live in coinvariants of a generic local system (H^2_c); positivity lets one eigenvalue be bounded by the whole family. Claim A's shape "|alpha|^k <= q^((k+3)/2)" is the shape of Katz's step 5 (power inequality, constant independent of the exponent).
- Different from Laumon ch. 4: no purity of FT, no weight induction w -> w+1, no reality criterion, no product formula; instead a k-fold iterated FT (alternating with twists by a rank-one sheaf) and a fibrewise vanishing statement (Claim B). Laumon applies FT once. No Plancherel in Laumon.
- Different from Katz's four lectures: Katz's family T over A^2 has big monodromy, positivity comes from L-functions of tensor powers of T tensor Tbar on curves, and the moment criterion is group theory. The route's family is the h-line with fixed squarefree f. The orthogonality device (Parseval over h) is Katz's Step 5 device, applied k-1 times along a path quadric rather than once on a four-variable diagonal.
- Elementary relatives: Kloosterman 1926 (4th moment, p^(3/4)) and Mordell's bound O(d q^(1-1/(2d))) (Kopparty notes https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sk1233/courses/finitefields-F19/polycharsum.pdf, OPENED, Theorem 1): moment methods that do not reach 1/2 by themselves. The route claims to reach 1/2 by letting the moment index grow (exponent 1/2 + 3/(2k)), which is the Rankin pattern, not the Mordell pattern (Mordell's exponent worsens with the moment index).
- Verdict. The route is a VARIANT within the method-of-families tradition. It is not a special case of Laumon's chapter 4 (different induction, no purity of FT) nor of Katz's four lectures (different family, no moment criterion, no L-function of tensor powers). I cannot exclude that it appears elsewhere (Kiehl-Weissauer, Katz Asterisque 79, Brylinski not opened). NOT ESTABLISHED: Claims A and B. Where to look for a hidden circularity (HINT, not a found error): both Laumon and Katz need weights or an L-function positivity input exactly for the control of the eigenvalues on the coinvariant part; in the route that control is Claim A's "fibre sum is a sum of powers with one sign, bounded by Parseval". The sources contain no weight-free theorem evaluating such a fibre sum.

## 4. Semicontinuity of the Swan conductor (CITED, OPENED)
